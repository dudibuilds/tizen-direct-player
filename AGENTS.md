# RU7100 Direct Player

## Standing decisions

- 2026-11-18 — Keep the TV app in this repository beside the existing Stremio integration. Reuse compatible interfaces without making that integration a playback dependency.
- 2026-11-18 — The Samsung RU7100 must fetch and play media directly from the source, including subtitles, seeking, and resume. The Mac may sync data and relay control commands. Casting, FFmpeg, transcoding, remuxing, and serving media on the Mac are outside the playback path.
- 2026-11-18 — Standard BitTorrent is in scope for version 1. Resolve Stremio-compatible sources and run the torrent engine on the TV. Prove playback with the Mac asleep, including an uncached seek, subtitles, and resume, before treating the requirement as met.
- 2026-11-18 — Use Samsung's hardware player for compatible media. Evaluate additional containers and codecs on the TV as needed against actual hardware and performance.
- 2026-11-18 — Expose application commands and playback status to agents through an authenticated API and MCP. Use the same player for remote and agent commands, with independent permissions for playback and recommendation updates.
- 2026-11-18 — Recommendations come from deterministic code that keeps itself current without agents; agents may optionally curate but nothing depends on them. The Netflix history import (on the Mac) only bootstraps the taste profile. After that the profile learns from in-app behaviour (plays, completion, abandons, rewatches, thumbs, "not interested") and the feed keeps surfacing new, relevant, playable titles. Keep watch state and the latest feed usable on the TV when the Mac is asleep and sync when it returns. Approach (user, 2026-11-18): no model training. A scheduled CLI pulls latest releases and related titles from free sources (Stremio catalog add-ons, Cinemeta, IMDb datasets; TMDB/Trakt when the user supplies keys), filters them with explicit rules against the taste profile and in-app feedback, and checks they are playable. An agent is only a fallback when the rule-based feed is too thin. Details: docs/recommendations.md.
- 2026-11-18 — Version 1 must work with the physical Samsung remote throughout playback. Provide play/pause, forward/backward seek, subtitle track and language selection, audio track and language selection, and subtitle size and timing offset. Keep these controls available in the on-TV player and validate the full flow on the RU7100. MCP and the remote use the same player commands.
- 2026-11-18 — During playback, the remote's center button opens the transport and settings overlay. With the overlay open it activates the focused control. Dedicated media buttons perform their labeled play, pause, and stop actions.
- 2026-11-18 — The TV app loads user-installed Stremio add-ons (e.g. Torrentio, OpenSubtitles) by manifest URL and from the user's Stremio account, and natively provides every Stremio feature of `samsung-stremio-spotify-mcp`: search, library and watch state, new episodes, library edits, play/resume/next episode, binge and stop-after, ordered queue, source ranking with language tiers and size caps, failover on stalled sources, and progress/watched sync to Stremio (Trakt optional). Spotify, TV power and Chromecast/DLNA targets stay in the old MCP. Mac-side remux/HLS fallbacks from the old MCP are not ported (see the direct-playback decision).
- 2026-11-18 — Subtitles (embedded and external) are drawn by one app subtitle layer with a per-user style: colour, outline, background box and opacity, position (raised when the overlay shows), weight, size, and ±30 s timing. The style lives in `core/Ui` `PlayerSettings` and is shared by the remote UI and the agent API.
- 2026-11-18 — Remember audio and subtitle language per show and apply them on the next episode. Global default is English audio and English subtitles (the preferred subtitle language is `Languages.Subtitles`); anime defaults to Japanese audio with English subtitles.
- 2026-11-18 — Develop and review the UI on the Mac first: `preview/` renders the same `core/Ui` view-models and layout in a browser with the keyboard as the remote. Use the TV only for hardware checks (decoding, remote, ElmSharp rendering).
- 2026-11-18 — UI quality bar: Netflix-level polish without Netflix branding. Animated focus and scrolling (no scrollbars; neighbouring rows stay visible), search-as-you-type across all installed catalog add-ons, readable source picker with quality/size/health badges, and trailer preview autoplay after a short idle on a focused title (sound and timing: see the trailer preview decision below).
- 2026-11-19 — Performance is part of every UI component: quick remote navigation (holding or flicking arrows between titles and rows) must stay smooth on the RU7100, with no stutter. New or redesigned components reuse painter objects instead of recreating them, never decode or load images on the UI thread during navigation, defer heavy work (backdrop swaps, trailers, pop-out cards) until focus rests, and are checked with frame timings on the TV, not only in the Mac preview.
- 2026-11-19 — Design-system changes (palette, accent, typography, shapes, focus style, screen layout, motion language) need the user's approval, normally through mocks first. The user approved the direction of the 2026-11-19 design mocks (ink base #0B0E14, amber accent #FFB23E for progress/selected/primary Play only, white focus, wordmark, gradients instead of bands, right-edge player sheets, real transport, four-step type ramp, two motion curves) and asked for the redesign to be done in place. New design directions beyond that still get mocked first.
- 2026-11-19 — Design language (tokens in `core/Ui/UiFrame.cs` `UiTheme` and `core/Ui/UiScale.cs`). Ink page #0B0E14, panels #151A22 / #1E2530, hairline #2A3340; text #FFFFFF, meta #A9B1BD, faint #7E8794. White (#FFFFFF ring on cards, #F2F2F2 fill with ink label on buttons and chips, #FFFFFF26 soft fill with a bold white label on list rows) always means focus. Amber #FFB23E means the viewer's state only: watch progress, the selected option, the primary Play button, best match; it is never a focus colour. Quality labels, chips and badges are neutral; green #5AD1A0 is health/ready/watched. Type ramp: Title 56, Head 40, Prose 34, Meta 28 (Display 72 for logo-less titles, Label 26 for badges; nothing smaller). Radii: card 14, panel 16, list row 12, badge 8, buttons and chips pill, edge sheets square. Player chrome is gradients over the picture, never bands; player panels are right-edge sheets on one dim layer. Motion: travel and scale on cubic-bezier(0.2, 0, 0, 1); fades ease-out in and ease-in out, exits shorter than entrances. Icons and soft shadows are `UiGlyphs` Image nodes ("glyph:name", "shadow:r:s") so both painters draw them: tinted PNG and 9-slice on the TV, SVG mask and CSS shadow in the preview.
- 2026-11-18 — No on-TV code hot reload: Samsung's launcher only runs DLLs inside the signed, installed package (`Assembly.Load(byte[])` and side-loaded DLLs are rejected). TV iteration uses `scripts/tv-dev.sh` (build → sign → install → relaunch → forward → wait for API); UI iteration happens in the Mac preview.
- 2026-11-18 — Tizen 5.0 ships Newtonsoft.Json 12.0.0.0 and always loads it, so the TV build stays on Newtonsoft.Json 12.0.x (`core/Core.props`).
- 2026-11-18 — TV development mode: a dev-only TV build paints frames and runs the player/torrent engine, while the app logic and UI run on the Mac and drive it over the LAN (media still flows source → TV). Release builds exclude it. Use it for fast UI/logic iteration on the real TV; reinstall only for painter/player changes.
- 2026-11-18 — The RU7100 cannot decode WebP. Cinemeta's image host (images.metahub.space) serves WebP by default but returns JPEG (posters, backgrounds) or PNG (logos) when the URL carries `?format=jpg`, so the TV requests that; WebP from other hosts is retried with a format hint, else the card shows its text title. No third-party image proxy.
- 2026-11-18 — On the RU7100, ElmSharp's elm `Image` widget loads files but never draws; the TV painter uses raw `EvasImage` for posters, shapes and gradients (verified with the on-TV test card, `TvUiDevFlags.Probe`).
- 2026-11-18 — Torrent playback on the TV uses a bounded rolling cache held in RAM (default 160 MB; the RU7100's app partition is 946 MB with ~100 MB free, so disk caching does not fit): keep a short window behind the playhead and a buffer ahead, evict played pieces, stop downloading when paused with the buffer full, re-fetch on far seeks. Any size of movie must play without filling storage. Disk mode (free-space checked) exists for devices with room; USB-stick cache and debrid services are possible later add-ons.
- 2026-11-19 — Torrent peer connectivity runs on two parallel tracks; uTP stays in scope. Track A tunes the current MonoTorrent engine: fixed listen port with UPnP/NAT-PMP, longer connect timeout, relaxed encryption, engine warm at launch, file head and tail fetched together, race cut once a winner is clear. Track B decides the uTP route: a native-library spike on the RU7100 with librqbit (Rust; has TCP+uTP, UPnP, streaming and pluggable in-memory storage) as the first candidate engine, called from the .NET app over a C ABI; if librqbit lacks something we need (including uTP), we add it rather than switch engines. Fallbacks only if Rust native code cannot run on the TV: the Go/anacrolix approach of torrserver-tizen, then libtorrent, then our own uTP in C#. Choose with before/after peer and start-time numbers from the TV log.
- 2026-11-19 — The RU7100 will not load native code from our package: DllImport of our own bundled .so fails with "failed to map segment from shared object" (lib/ and bin/ alike), the packaged helper executable got "Permission denied", and the installer rejects managed code that imports system libraries such as libc.so.6 directly. So librqbit, libtorrent and Go engines are not usable with our signing profile. Decision (user, 2026-11-19): write uTP (BEP 29) ourselves in managed C# and plug it into MonoTorrent; develop and verify it on the Mac against real uTP clients, with TV checks at milestones. No custom native loaders.
- 2026-11-19 — Assume a fast home connection (the reference setup has ~1 Gbps down, ~100 Mbps up). Source choice and racing must avoid bottlenecks: the TV's own network link and CPU are likely the limit (2019 Samsung, often 100 Mbps Ethernet), so size the race to measured TV throughput, prefer the best quality whose bitrate leaves headroom under it, and allow generous upload (tit-for-tat) since upstream has room.
- 2026-11-19 — Torrent speed optimizations (source health checks, next-episode prefetch, deadline piece picking, peer selection, etc.) reach the TV only after they are proven on the Mac with the start-time harness and show a real improvement in start time or stalls; otherwise they stay off the TV build. uTP ships to the TV as soon as it is done, ahead of these optimizations.
- 2026-11-19 — Playback speed has one acceptance test: choose an episode or movie and it is playable in under 10 s, aiming for 3-5 s. Check it with a few runs on a few real titles; no large test matrices. Changes that don't move starts toward that target aren't worth shipping.
- 2026-11-18 — Size the whole UI for 10-foot viewing (the user sits far from a 55" TV): large text and posters everywhere; nothing smaller than TV-safe minimums. The current UI is too small — scale it up app-wide, then tune.
- 2026-11-19 — Trailers on the TV come from IMDb (official trailers as full-length 1080p/720p H.264+AAC MP4 via IMDb's GraphQL `primaryVideos`, played directly by AVPlay; limited personal non-commercial use per IMDb's terms). Titles without an IMDb trailer show still art. Never below 480p. Code: `core/Trailers`.
- 2026-11-18 — Trailer previews follow Netflix's TV behaviour: start after ~2.5 s dwell only when the viewer pressed a remote key within the last ~10 s (never on launch or while the TV sits idle), play WITH sound faded in to a reduced preview level (default 60% of player volume), fade out on any navigation, play once (no loop), no on-screen sound hint; Play/Pause always plays the focused title (user, 2026-11-19), it never toggles preview sound — sound is controlled by the "Preview sound" setting; settings "Autoplay previews" and "Preview sound" (both default on). Never overlap real playback. Changed 2026-11-19 after the usability review (user agreed): on the title page the automatic preview plays in the header behind the page, so the actions and synopsis stay on screen and the first key both stops it and acts; the full-screen trailer view there opens only from the Trailer button.
- 2026-11-19 — Row cards pop out with their trailer, like Netflix's web hover card: when focus rests on a row title for ~1-1.5 s, the card enlarges in place (not full screen) with the trailer playing in its top and Play / My List / rating / more-info controls plus facts and genres below; neighbouring rows stay visible and any navigation shrinks it back immediately so browsing trailers stays fast. Same sound, idle and decoder rules as the trailer preview decision; only one trailer plays at a time (card trailer replaces the hero's). Pop delay ~1.2 s. Changed 2026-11-19 after the usability review (user agreed): OK means the same on every screen, it opens the title page; only Continue watching cards resume on OK, and Play/Pause always plays the focused title, even while its trailer is sounding (user, 2026-11-19). On a popped card Down steps into its button row (Play / My List / Rate / More info; Left/Right move, OK activates, Up returns, Down goes on to the next row); Left/Right/Up on the card itself still shrink it and move on at once, and focus is never trapped. While a card is popped the billboard text steps aside and the row title rides above the card; a Top 10 card shows its rank as a badge. Code: `core/Ui/UiLayoutPopCard.cs`, `HomeViewModel`.
- 2026-11-19 — Discovery must not depend on title data bundled in the app (keep the app small). Browse and other discovery screens page through online catalogs (Cinemeta and the user's Stremio connection/add-ons, other free public services) on demand with no fixed title cap, and narrow them with the app's own filters and source checks. Code: `core/Ui/BrowseViewModel.cs`.
- 2026-11-20 — Anything published (a public repo, commits, packages, posts) identifies the author only as "dudi" (GitHub `dudibuilds`, its noreply email), never the full name, a personal email or the Mac's host name. The package id `org.dudi.ru7100probe` stays. A public copy is prepared locally for the user's review first; nothing is pushed without their go-ahead.
